## SRI ARC Journal

The strongest new local finding is **Journal 32800, "DARPA Comments on AUTODIN II Phase I Program Memorandum," dated June 23, 1975 in its heading and entered June 22**. The journal attribution identifies David C. Russell. It is a set of comments and recommendations; this copy does not establish their formal adoption.

[Retained SRI Journal volume](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2017/10/102775961-05-01-acc.pdf), PDF pp. 97-101, internally numbered pp. 2-5 followed by the journal attribution page. All five pages have now been inspected visually across the two batches. Some line endings are clipped in the preserved scan.

- **Protocol change and its costs:** paragraph 9a, printed p. 5 / PDF p. 100, recommends a mechanism for changing host protocols that weighs new capabilities against implementation costs to network users. This directly supports the historical account's concern with concurrent development and operating commitments.
- **Security changes network structure:** paragraphs 6-7, especially printed p. 4 / PDF p. 99, explain how requirements for secure switches and cleared personnel favor a small number of highly connected sites. The comments argue that end-to-end encryption would permit a different physical and operating arrangement. This is a concrete connection between an operational requirement and an architectural choice, rather than an attribution inferred from a later packet field.
- **Earlier alternative to a new switching system:** paragraphs 3-4 and 8 recommend learning through an ARPANET-derived military network while continuing work on encryption, reliability, and host protocols. Compare this proposal with the 1982 DDN selection, then investigate why the earlier proposal was not adopted on the same terms. Similarity between proposals is not a demonstrated continuous decision chain.

The memorandum itself supplies precise next targets: the **January 9, 1975 D. ARPA memorandum to DTACCS, "AUTODIN II, Integrated Data Network, Subsystem Project Plan 2-74,"** and the June 1975 DTACCS guidance memorandum to the Telecommunications Council. Reference a's day is clipped at the right edge of PDF p. 97; do not supply an exact day from this scan. Paragraph 5, printed p. 3 / PDF p. 98, cites a June 1975 Network Analysis Corporation study in support of distributed switching, lower communications costs, and simpler host interfaces. These are the comments' claims about the study, not an independent reading of it. Neither memorandum nor that study was recovered in the bounded local and public search.

The other two Journal leads are identified and checked against their original pages:

- [Journal 32190, memorandum of agreement on transferring ARPANET management to DCA](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2017/10/102775942-05-01-acc.pdf), entered March 25, 1975, with an NLS source timestamp of February 28. The complete journal item is PDF pp. 111-117, printed pp. 2-7 followed by attribution p. 1. It proposes transfer effective July 1, 1975. Clauses k-l, printed p. 6 / PDF p. 115, allow coordinated experiments and DCA-authorized AUTODIN II concept testing on ARPANET. Printed p. 7 / PDF p. 116 has typed names, **blank signatures and blank dates**. The title, experimental clauses, signature page, and attribution were inspected visually; the remaining body was read through extracted text. This establishes a proposed arrangement for overlapping operational and experimental work, not an executed agreement or completed AUTODIN II tests.
- [Journal 36044, *Proposal for Research No. ISU 76-136: Network Operating System Study*](unique/sri-journal-36044/), NLS timestamp July 16, 1976, responds to RADC RFP F30602-76-R-0299. The raw NLS listing's pp. 1 and 10 / PDF pp. 113 and 122 were inspected visually. Its qualifications name Postel as principal investigator for TCP/THP specification contract **DCA100-76-C-0034**, February through June 1976. This is a contemporaneous contract description embedded in a different proposal. It is not the AUTODIN II contract or its statement of work. Those documents, reviews, and correspondence remain targets.

The public IEN text copies also sometimes omit scanned attachments. The notebook source checks distinguish complete visual reading of a page from discovery through OCR.
